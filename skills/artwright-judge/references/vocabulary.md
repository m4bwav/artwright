# Style vocabulary

Style terms with one-line visual definitions, for naming what an image shows and for writing the prompt that asks for it.

Checked 2026-10-21 · Back to [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md)

Verification: the definitions are descriptive craft usage, `verified: no` as a whole (no row cites a fetched source); the dead-words section cites its evidence.

How to use it:

- **To describe an image:** find the definition that matches what you can point at in the frame, then use the term. Name the evidence ("hard raking light from frame left") as well as the term.
- **To prompt:** on LLM-encoder models (Z-Image, FLUX.2, Qwen-Image, Wan, MiniMax H3, the closed models) the definition often prompts better than the bare term. Write "one hard light from the left, deep shadow masses" rather than "chiaroscuro" alone, or use both.
- Whether a term lands on a given model is untested. No study covers movement or technique words on current open models, so log what works in the per-model keyword log with evidence (R-20261021-9).
- Living artists are never listed as style terms. To get a living artist's look, use the traits recipe at the end.

## Medium and technique

- **oil paint**: rich saturated colour, blended or thick strokes, glossy depth, visible canvas weave
- **impasto**: paint laid on thick enough to stand up from the surface, ridged strokes catching light
- **glazing**: thin transparent layers built up, glowing deep colour, soft luminous transitions
- **alla prima**: wet-into-wet painting finished in one session, loose confident strokes, fresh edges
- **sfumato**: edges dissolved into smoky gradients with no hard lines, soft veiled form
- **tempera**: egg-based paint, matte, fine hatched strokes, crisp edges, pale clear colour
- **fresco**: pigment in wet plaster, chalky matte pastel colour, flat broad areas, slight cracking
- **gouache**: opaque matte watercolour, flat even colour fields, crisp shapes, chalky finish
- **watercolour**: transparent washes, white paper glowing through, soft blooms, pooled edges
- **wet-on-wet**: colour dropped into wet paper, feathered bleeding edges, blurred blends
- **dry brush**: stiff almost dry brush, broken scratchy strokes showing paper grain
- **ink wash**: diluted ink in graded greys, brush-loaded tonal pools, sumi-e economy
- **pen and ink**: crisp black lines on white, hatching for tone, no colour
- **cross-hatching**: layered crossing line sets building tone, darker where denser
- **stippling**: tone made from dots, denser dots for darker areas
- **charcoal**: smudgy rich blacks, soft gradients, dusty grain, erased highlights
- **graphite**: silvery grey pencil, fine controlled shading, slight sheen in darks
- **pastel**: soft powdery colour, chalky strokes, visible paper tooth, bright light-catching pigment
- **oil pastel**: waxy thick crayon colour, smeared blends, scraped textures
- **coloured pencil**: layered fine strokes, waxy burnished colour, paper grain showing
- **marker rendering**: streaky flat alcohol-marker layers, crisp edges, design-sketch look
- **acrylic**: fast-drying plastic paint, flat or textured, bright clean colour, hard edges
- **airbrush**: perfectly smooth sprayed gradients, soft edges, glossy chrome-and-skin finish
- **spray paint**: aerosol colour, soft overspray halos, drips, stencil edges, wall texture
- **collage**: cut and pasted papers and photos, layered edges, mixed sources
- **photomontage**: cut photographs combined into one jarring image, visible seams
- **mosaic**: image built from small tesserae, grout lines, faceted colour
- **stained glass**: jewel-coloured translucent panes, thick black lead lines, backlit glow
- **tapestry**: woven threads, soft pixel-like weave grid, muted dyed colour
- **embroidery**: raised thread stitches, satin sheen, fabric ground showing
- **papercut**: layered cut paper silhouettes, crisp edges, cast shadows between layers
- **sculpture, marble**: smooth cool white stone, subsurface glow, carved drapery
- **bronze**: dark metallic sculpture, green-brown patina, polished highlights on wear points
- **claymation**: plasticine figures, thumbprints, slightly jittery handmade surfaces
- **diorama**: miniature built scene, shallow staging, model-scale props
- **tilt-shift miniature**: real scene made to look like a model by a narrow focus band and high viewpoint
- **cyanotype**: Prussian-blue photographic print, white silhouettes, soft sun-print edges
- **daguerreotype**: mirror-like early photo, silvery tones, extreme detail, stiff posed subjects
- **tintype**: dark metal-plate photo, murky edges, high-contrast faces, scratches
- **wet plate collodion**: swirling edge artefacts, silver tones, blue-sensitive skin rendering
- **Polaroid**: square instant print, white border, soft colour shifts, slight haze
- **double exposure**: two images overlaid translucently, one silhouette filled by another
- **long exposure**: motion turned to streaks and silk, static parts sharp
- **macro photo**: extreme close-up, tiny details huge, razor-thin focus
- **street photography**: candid people in public, available light, off-balance moments
- **documentary photo**: unposed truthful scene, natural light, context-rich framing
- **fashion editorial**: styled model, strong pose, designed lighting, magazine polish
- **product photo**: object isolated on clean ground, controlled reflections, sharp detail

## Movement and period shorthand

- **prehistoric cave art**: ochre and charcoal animals on rock, outline and flat fill, no ground line
- **ancient Egyptian**: flat profile figures, frontal eye, registers, hierarchical scale, hieroglyph bands
- **Greek black-figure**: black silhouettes on orange clay, incised detail lines
- **Greek red-figure**: orange figures reserved on black ground, fine painted line detail
- **Byzantine**: gold ground, frontal stiff saints, large eyes, flat iconic space
- **Gothic**: elongated graceful figures, pointed arches, gold, jewel colour, S-curve poses
- **illuminated manuscript**: gold leaf, decorated initials, vine borders, flat bright pigment
- **Early Renaissance**: linear perspective, calm clear light, solid modelled figures, ordered space
- **High Renaissance**: balanced pyramidal groups, idealised anatomy, soft modelling, harmony
- **Northern Renaissance**: oil-glaze detail, crisp textures, every object rendered, cool light
- **Mannerism**: elongated twisting figures, acid colour, crowded unstable space
- **Baroque**: dramatic diagonals, theatrical light, motion, rich dark colour, emotion at peak
- **Caravaggesque**: tenebrism, figures lit by one beam from darkness, gritty real models
- **Dutch Golden Age**: quiet interiors, window light, precise textures, muted earth palette
- **vanitas**: still life of skulls, candles, wilting flowers, reminders of death
- **Rococo**: pastel colour, frothy curves, playful aristocrats, light feathery brushwork
- **Neoclassicism**: crisp outline, cool marble-like figures, frieze-like order, stoic poses
- **Romanticism**: sublime nature, storms, drama, glowing skies, small figures against vastness
- **Hudson River School**: luminous panoramic American landscape, golden light, fine detail
- **Pre-Raphaelite**: jewel-bright colour, minute detail, medieval themes, flowing hair
- **Realism (19th c.)**: ordinary people and labour, earthy palette, unidealised bodies
- **Impressionism**: broken dabs of pure colour, outdoor light, soft edges, fleeting moments
- **Pointillism**: tiny dots of pure colour mixing in the eye, shimmering surface
- **Post-Impressionism**: bold colour, visible structure, expressive stroke, simplified form
- **Symbolism**: dreamlike mystical scenes, pale figures, myth, mood over realism
- **Art Nouveau**: whiplash curves, flowing hair and plants, ornamental borders, flat colour
- **Arts and Crafts**: hand-made look, dense botanical pattern, muted natural dyes
- **Fauvism**: wild unnatural colour, flat areas, loose energetic stroke
- **Expressionism**: distorted forms, harsh colour, jagged lines, emotional intensity
- **German Expressionist woodcut**: angular black slashes, raw gouged marks, stark contrast
- **Cubism**: subjects broken into faceted planes, multiple viewpoints at once, muted palette
- **Analytic Cubism**: monochrome browns and greys, dense small facets, near-abstract
- **Synthetic Cubism**: flatter bright shapes, collage elements, lettering
- **Futurism**: speed lines, repeated motion, fractured dynamic forms
- **Suprematism**: floating basic geometric shapes on white, few colours
- **Constructivism**: diagonal red, black and white geometry, photomontage, bold sans type
- **De Stijl**: black grid, primary colour rectangles, white ground, right angles only
- **Bauhaus**: clean geometric forms, primary colours, sans-serif type, function first
- **Dada**: absurd collage, random type, anti-art mockery
- **Surrealism**: dream logic, impossible juxtapositions, precise rendering of the irrational
- **Art Deco**: streamlined symmetry, sunbursts, chevrons, gold and black, luxury geometry
- **Social Realism**: workers and struggle, heavy forms, muted earnest colour
- **Mexican muralism**: monumental figures, bold earth colour, political narrative on walls
- **Abstract Expressionism**: large gestural or colour-field canvases, no subject, raw scale
- **action painting**: dripped, flung, splattered paint, energetic tangles
- **Colour Field**: big flat or soaked fields of colour, quiet edges
- **Pop Art**: commercial imagery, flat bright colour, Ben-Day dots, bold outlines
- **Op Art**: black-white or colour patterns that vibrate, warp or flicker
- **Minimalism**: few simple forms, industrial finish, emptiness
- **Photorealism**: painting indistinguishable from a photo, chrome, reflections, glass
- **ukiyo-e**: Japanese woodblock, flat colour, bold outline, stylised waves and figures, no cast shadows
- **sumi-e**: black ink brush painting, few strokes, much empty space
- **Chinese shan shui**: misty layered mountains and water, ink and light colour, vertical scroll
- **Persian miniature**: flat bright colour, patterned tiles and textiles, high tilted ground plane
- **Mughal miniature**: fine detail, gold borders, profile figures, court scenes
- **Aboriginal dot painting**: patterned dots and concentric circles, earth colours, map-like
- **Celtic knotwork**: interlaced endless bands, spirals, zoomorphic ornament
- **Islamic geometric**: tessellating star patterns, symmetry, arabesque, no figures

## Lighting

- **chiaroscuro**: strong light-dark modelling, one hard source, deep shadow masses
- **tenebrism**: extreme chiaroscuro, most of the frame black, subjects spot-lit
- **Rembrandt lighting**: key high at 45 degrees, small light triangle on the shadow cheek
- **butterfly lighting**: key high and frontal, small shadow under the nose, glamour look
- **split lighting**: key at 90 degrees, half the face lit, half dark
- **rim light**: thin bright edge outlining the subject from behind
- **backlight**: source behind subject, glowing edges, silhouette tendency, flare
- **silhouette**: subject black against bright background, shape only
- **contre-jour**: shooting into the light, haze, flare, translucent edges
- **key light**: the main source that sets direction and shadow
- **fill light**: softer secondary light lifting shadows
- **contact shadow**: the small dark shadow where an object meets the ground; without it things float
- **Fresnel reflection**: glossy surfaces mirror more at grazing angles (a floor shining toward the far end)
- **high-key**: bright, low contrast, few shadows, airy
- **low-key**: mostly dark, high contrast, small lit areas, moody
- **hard light**: small or distant source, crisp shadow edges
- **soft light**: large diffuse source, gradual shadow transitions
- **raking light**: low angle across a surface, texture exaggerated
- **top light**: overhead source, eye sockets in shadow, harsh
- **under-lighting**: from below, spooky inverted shadows
- **practical light**: visible lamps, candles or screens in frame lighting the scene
- **motivated light**: light that matches a believable in-scene source
- **candlelight**: warm flickering orange pools, deep falloff
- **firelight**: warm orange underlight, dancing shadows, ember glow
- **window light**: soft directional daylight from one side, gradual falloff into the room
- **north light**: cool even skylight from a window with no direct sun, painter's studio
- **golden hour**: low warm sun, long shadows, glowing edges
- **blue hour**: after sunset, deep blue ambient, warm artificial lights glowing
- **overcast**: soft shadowless daylight, muted colour, even exposure
- **harsh noon sun**: overhead hard light, short dark shadows, bleached highlights
- **dappled light**: sun broken by leaves into spots on the subject
- **god rays**: visible light shafts through haze, dust or trees
- **volumetric light**: light visible in the air via fog or dust
- **caustics**: rippling light patterns from water or glass
- **subsurface glow**: light passing through skin, wax, leaves; warm translucent edges
- **neon**: saturated pink, cyan, purple tubes, coloured reflections on wet surfaces
- **fluorescent**: flat greenish overhead office light, institutional
- **moonlight**: cool blue low light, soft shadows, desaturated
- **bioluminescence**: soft blue-green glow from organisms in darkness
- **lens flare**: bright artefact streaks and circles from a light hitting the lens
- **Hollywood three-point**: key, fill and back light, clean modelled subject
- **film noir lighting**: hard low key, venetian-blind shadows, smoke

## Composition and camera

- **rule of thirds**: subject on a third line or crossing, not centred (a habit, not a law)
- **centred composition**: subject dead centre, formal, static, iconic
- **symmetry**: mirrored halves, formal, calm or uncanny
- **asymmetrical balance**: unequal masses balanced by weight, colour or space
- **leading lines**: roads, rails, edges drawing the eye to the subject
- **diagonal composition**: main lines on the diagonal, energy and movement
- **triangular composition**: elements forming a triangle, stable grouping
- **S-curve**: winding path or form, graceful flow through the frame
- **golden spiral**: claimed spiral arrangement; no evidence it pleases more (describe, never justify with it)
- **frame within a frame**: doorway, window or arch enclosing the subject
- **negative space**: large empty area around a small subject
- **fill the frame**: subject crops edge to edge, no breathing room
- **foreground interest**: object close to the lens leading into the scene
- **layered depth**: distinct foreground, middle ground and background planes
- **atmospheric perspective**: distant things paler, bluer, lower contrast
- **linear perspective**: parallel lines converging to vanishing points
- **one-point perspective**: lines converging to a single central point, corridor look
- **two-point perspective**: building corner toward viewer, two vanishing points
- **repetition and pattern**: repeating elements, rhythm, one break for interest
- **visual weight**: where the eye lands first: contrast, faces, saturated colour
- **focal point**: the one area the composition leads to
- **lost and found edges**: some edges crisp, some melting into the ground
- **lead room**: space in front of a subject facing or moving that way
- **horizon placement**: low horizon gives sky drama, high gives ground detail
- **extreme close-up**: a detail fills the frame, an eye or a hand
- **close-up**: face fills the frame
- **medium close-up**: head and shoulders
- **medium shot**: waist up
- **full shot**: whole body head to toe
- **wide shot**: figure small in an environment
- **establishing shot**: very wide view setting the location
- **over-the-shoulder**: past one person's shoulder to another
- **POV**: from a character's eyes
- **eye-level**: camera at subject's eye height, neutral
- **low angle**: camera below looking up, subject looms
- **high angle**: camera above looking down, subject small
- **worm's-eye view**: from the ground straight up
- **bird's-eye view**: straight down from above
- **Dutch angle**: camera rolled, tilted horizon, unease
- **isometric view**: 3D with no vanishing point, 30-degree axes, game-map look
- **orthographic view**: flat front, side or top with no perspective
- **static shot**: camera does not move
- **pan**: camera turns left or right on its axis
- **tilt**: camera turns up or down on its axis
- **dolly**: camera moves toward or away from the subject
- **zoom**: focal length changes, camera stays; flattens depth on the way in
- **crane shot**: camera sweeps up or down over the scene
- **arc shot**: camera circles the subject
- **tracking shot**: camera follows a moving subject
- **handheld**: slight shake and drift, documentary immediacy
- **whip pan**: very fast pan blurring into the next view
- **dolly zoom**: dolly and zoom in opposite directions, background stretches
- **rack focus**: focus shifts from one plane to another
- **aerial shot**: drone or helicopter view from above, moving
- **ultra-wide lens**: 14-24 mm, exaggerated depth, stretched edges
- **wide-angle lens**: 24-35 mm, environment included, slight edge stretch
- **normal lens**: about 50 mm, natural perspective
- **portrait lens**: 85-135 mm, flattering compression, soft background
- **telephoto**: 200 mm and up, flattened stacked planes, distant subject close
- **fisheye**: circular or strongly curved distortion, near 180 degrees
- **anamorphic**: wide 2.39:1 frame, oval bokeh, horizontal blue flares
- **macro lens**: life-size detail, very shallow focus
- **shallow depth of field**: only one plane sharp, background melted
- **deep focus**: everything sharp from near to far
- **bokeh**: quality of out-of-focus blur, discs of light
- **motion blur**: moving parts streaked, implies speed
- **film grain**: fine random noise from film, organic texture
- **halation**: red-orange glow around highlights on film
- **chromatic aberration**: colour fringes on high-contrast edges
- **vignette**: corners darker than centre
- **Kodak Portra 400 look**: warm natural skin, soft pastel colour, fine grain
- **Kodachrome look**: saturated reds and blues, deep contrast, vintage slide
- **Fuji Velvia look**: hyper-saturated landscapes, vivid greens, deep blues
- **Tri-X black-and-white**: gritty grain, punchy contrast, reportage
- **CineStill 800T look**: tungsten-balanced night film, red halation around lights
- **2000s digicam**: harsh on-camera flash, slight blur, compressed colours, date stamp feel
- **VHS look**: smeared colour, scan lines, tracking noise, soft low resolution
- **35 mm film**: cinematic grain, rich colour, theatrical film look
- **aspect ratio**: frame shape: 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9, 2.39:1, 9:16 vertical

## Colour

- **monochromatic**: tints and shades of one hue
- **analogous**: neighbouring hues, harmonious and calm
- **complementary**: opposite hues side by side, vivid contrast
- **triadic**: three evenly spaced hues, lively balance
- **warm palette**: reds, oranges, yellows dominate
- **cool palette**: blues, greens, violets dominate
- **muted palette**: greyed low-saturation colour
- **pastel palette**: pale light high-value colours
- **jewel tones**: deep saturated emerald, sapphire, ruby, amethyst
- **earth tones**: ochres, umbers, siennas, olive, terracotta
- **limited palette**: very few pigments, often three or four, unified colour
- **Zorn palette**: ochre, vermilion, black and white; warm skin, greyed blues
- **neon palette**: electric pinks, cyans, limes on dark
- **duotone**: image in two inks, often a dark and a bright
- **sepia**: brown monochrome, aged photo
- **black and white**: no colour, tone only
- **high contrast**: deep blacks and bright whites, few mid-tones
- **low contrast**: compressed tones, hazy and soft
- **saturated**: intense pure colour
- **desaturated**: drained colour close to grey
- **bleach bypass**: low saturation, high contrast, silvery gritty grade
- **teal and orange**: warm skin against cyan shadows (a default AI and blockbuster grade)
- **day for night**: daylight footage graded dark blue to read as night
- **colour temperature**: warm orange (low Kelvin) to cool blue (high Kelvin) cast
- **colour accent**: one saturated element in an otherwise muted frame
- **value structure**: arrangement of lights and darks that reads in greyscale
- **notan**: two-value light-dark pattern of a composition
- **tonal range**: spread from darkest dark to lightest light
- **hex-specified colour**: an exact colour written as a hex code tied to a named object (FLUX.2)

## Line and shape

- **contour line**: single line following the edge of form
- **gesture drawing**: quick lines catching pose and movement, not detail
- **line weight variation**: thick lines in shadow or near, thin in light or far
- **uniform line**: same-width line everywhere, technical or ligne claire
- **calligraphic line**: swelling and tapering brush line
- **scratchy line**: broken nervous hatching, sketchbook energy
- **clean line art**: tidy unbroken lines, ready for colour
- **outline-free**: forms defined by colour and value only
- **silhouette readability**: shape recognisable as a solid black shape
- **geometric shapes**: circles, squares, triangles, hard and designed
- **organic shapes**: irregular flowing natural forms
- **shape language**: circles friendly, squares stable, triangles dangerous
- **angular**: sharp corners and jagged edges
- **rounded**: soft curves, bubbly forms
- **elongated**: stretched vertical proportions, elegance or unease
- **squash and stretch**: forms deforming with motion, cartoon elasticity
- **flat shapes**: no modelling, colour areas only
- **hard edges**: crisp boundaries between areas
- **soft edges**: gradual transitions
- **radial**: elements spreading from a centre
- **grid**: regular rows and columns

- **patina**: aged colour change on metal or wood
- **rust**: orange flaking corrosion on iron
- **weathered**: sun-bleached, worn, chipped surfaces
- **distressed**: deliberately aged, scuffed, faded print
- **grunge**: dirty scratched overlays, stains, splatters
- **glossy**: sharp reflections, wet look
- **matte**: no shine, diffuse surface
- **metallic**: specular highlights, environment reflected

- **iridescent**: hue shifting with angle, oil-slick rainbow
- **pearlescent**: soft milky shimmer
- **translucent**: light passes through, form blurred behind
- **frosted glass**: diffused blurry see-through
- **wet surface**: dark saturated material, puddle reflections
- **leather**: grained hide, creases, worn edges
- **wood grain**: flowing lines and knots
- **stone**: rough or polished mineral surface, speckle, veining
- **skin pores**: fine pores, peach fuzz, uneven tone (fights plastic skin)
- **fur**: dense directional strands, soft edges

- **risograph grain**: speckled soy-ink, slight misregistration

## Rendering and 3D

- **photoreal render**: physically based materials and light indistinguishable from photo
- **PBR materials**: albedo, roughness, metalness maps; consistent realistic surfaces
- **ray tracing**: accurate reflections, refraction, soft shadows
- **global illumination**: light bouncing between surfaces, colour bleed
- **ambient occlusion**: soft darkening in creases and contacts
- **subsurface scattering**: light glowing through skin, wax, marble
- **HDRI lighting**: environment map light, realistic reflections
- **clay render**: grey untextured material, form only
- **wireframe**: polygon edges visible
- **low poly**: few faceted polygons, flat shaded
- **flat shading**: each face one colour, faceted look
- **toon shading**: hard-banded cel lighting on 3D, outlines
- **cel-shaded 3D**: 3D that reads as 2D animation, flat colour bands
- **NPR render**: non-photoreal, painterly or sketch shaders on 3D
- **voxel art**: model built from cubes, blocky
- **isometric 3D diorama**: small floating cut-out scene, orthographic, soft shadows
- **claymorphism**: soft rounded inflated 3D shapes, pastel, matte
- **glassmorphism**: frosted translucent panels, blur behind
- **chrome render**: mirror metal, environment reflections, high contrast
- **octane-style render**: glossy, saturated, heavy bloom (a cliché; describe the light instead)
- **bloom**: glow bleeding around bright areas
- **depth of field render**: simulated lens blur in 3D
- **turntable render**: object on a rotating plinth, neutral background
- **orthographic render**: no perspective, technical views
- **cutaway**: object sliced open to show inside
- **hard-surface**: precise mechanical forms, bevels, panel lines
- **game engine real-time**: clean PBR, slight aliasing, screen-space effects
- **PS1-era 3D**: low poly, wobbly textures, no filtering, affine warping
- **matte painting**: painted background extension, photo-real vista

## Pixel and game art

- **pixel art**: image built on a visible square grid, deliberate pixels, limited palette
- **1-bit**: two colours only, dithered tones
- **8-bit**: very small sprites, few colours per sprite, NES-like
- **16-bit**: larger sprites, richer palettes, SNES and Genesis era
- **limited palette (pixel)**: fixed small set of colours, often 4-32
- **dithering**: checker or pattern mixing two colours to fake a third
- **colour ramp**: graded sequence of shades for one material, hue-shifted
- **hue shifting**: shadows shift toward cool, highlights toward warm
- **anti-aliasing (pixel)**: hand-placed in-between pixels smoothing edges
- **jaggies**: stair-step unsmoothed edges
- **pillow shading**: shading from the outline inward, flat blobby (a fault)
- **banding**: parallel lines of shades following the outline (a fault)
- **selective outline (selout)**: outline coloured by the adjacent fill
- **sprite**: single character or object image on transparency
- **sprite sheet**: frames laid on a grid for animation
- **tileset**: modular tiles that join seamlessly
- **seamless tile**: texture repeating with no visible seam
- **parallax background**: layers scrolling at different speeds
- **top-down**: view straight down or slightly angled, RPG maps
- **side-scroller**: side view, platform layout

## Dead magic words

Quality boosters from the CLIP era (SD1.5, SDXL): words added to lift "quality" without describing anything. Oppenlaender's taxonomy names them as a modifier class ("quality boosters", "magic terms", arXiv 2204.13988). On current LLM-encoder models they show no documented benefit, and vendor guides ask for concrete description instead:

- **FLUX.2** (Black Forest Labs guide): name a specific camera, lens, film stock or era rather than "professional photo".
- **Gemini image models (Nano Banana)**: Google's "golden rule" asks for natural sentences rather than keyword stacks.
- **Z-Image, Wan, MiniMax H3**: they read prose through LLM encoders (Qwen3, umT5, Qwen3-VL).

No controlled study measures these words on these models, so "dead" means no evidence of value, not proof of harm. Log any counter-evidence.

The list:

- masterpiece
- best quality, high quality, top quality
- ultra detailed, highly detailed, intricate details (unless the details are named)
- 8k, 4k, 16k, UHD, ultra HD, high resolution
- trending on artstation
- award-winning, award winning photo
- professional photo, professional photography
- hyperrealistic, hyper-detailed, photorealistic (when used as boosters; name the camera and light instead)
- unreal engine, octane render (clichés that pull a glossy CG look)
- sharp focus (fine on FLUX.2 as a positive rewrite of "no blur"; empty as a booster)
- beautiful, stunning, amazing, epic, breathtaking
- cinematic (alone; name the lens, light and grade)
- volumetric lighting (alone; say where the light comes from)
- weight syntax such as `(word:1.3)`, `((word))` and `[word]`: SD front-end conventions, not read as weights by plain LLM encoders (check the front-end)
- tag-list negatives such as "worst quality, low quality, bad anatomy" on models where negatives do nothing (Z-Image Turbo, FLUX.2)

Exception: Qwen-Image's own model card appends a "positive magic" suffix (", Ultra HD, 4K, cinematic composition."). On that model, follow the vendor. Recheck it on Qwen-Image 2.x.

## Living artist → traits

A prompt that names a living artist gets the name replaced by the traits that make the look, with one line saying so. The name is never put back. Why: artist names are recognisable in outputs (arXiv 2507.18633), the US Copyright Office's Part 3 report (pre-publication, May 2025) lists living-artist style guardrails and market dilution, and OpenAI's image tool has refused living-artist styles since March 2025.

Recipe. Fill each slot from what the work shows, using terms from this file:

1. **Medium and technique**: what it looks made with (gouache, linocut, digital painterly, cel shading).
2. **Palette**: hue family, saturation, value key, any signature accent colour.
3. **Line and shape**: line weight, outline or lineless, shape language, proportions.
4. **Light**: direction, hardness, key (high or low), colour temperature.
5. **Subject and composition habits**: what it depicts, typical framing, negative space, viewpoint.
6. **Era or movement kinship**: the nearest movement or period, named as an influence rather than an identity.
7. **Surface**: grain, paper, print texture, finish.

Worked example. Brief: "a fox in a winter forest in the style of [a living children's-book illustrator]". The agent looks at the illustrator's published work (or asks the user to describe it) and writes:

> A red fox standing in a snowy birch forest at dusk. Gouache on cold-press paper, matte opaque colour with visible dry-brush texture. Limited palette of slate blue, warm cream and one rust-orange accent on the fox. Rounded simple shapes, no outlines, small dark dot eyes. Soft overcast light, low contrast, high-key snow. Fox small in the lower third with wide negative space above, eye-level view. Mid-century picture-book sensibility, slight paper grain.

One line to the user: "Replaced the artist's name with the traits of the look (artwright does not prompt with living artists' names)."
